01
Section 01
Problem Statement

Loyalty programs find out a customer is slipping weeks after it starts, and then respond by discounting everyone. Reacting late costs money, and so does discounting people who were going to buy anyway.

The Context

A retail loyalty program exists to hold on to customers who would otherwise drift away, and to earn sales that would not have happened anyway.

The Gap

The signal arrives on a monthly churn report, weeks after the behavior changed. By then the response is a scramble: blanket discounts and bonus points to everyone, with no view of what each one costs.

The Impact

Profit goes to shoppers who would have bought anyway. Reward liability lands on the balance sheet unforecast. And brand partners refuse to co-fund without proof of incremental sales.

The Solve

Catch the drift in buying cadence as it happens, respond with an offer that has a budget cap built into it, and hold back a lookalike comparison group so the new profit can be proven to Finance and to brand partners.

02
Section 02
Strategy

Core Strategic Shifts

From Late Reports to Instant Action

Act on slipping customers the moment their buying habit breaks, instead of finding out in a monthly or quarterly report.

From Wasted Discounts to Protected Margins

Send targeted offers with strict budget limits attached, instead of throwing discounts and points at everyone.

From Guesswork to Causal Proof

Every campaign holds back a matched comparison group, so the lift you report to Finance is lift you can prove.

From Data Science Bottlenecks to Agentic Workflows

Marketers can set up and launch safe tests guided by the agent, with no data science backlog slowing things down.

Strategic Data Considerations

  • Data Readiness. The agent cannot trigger anything safely until identity is resolved, the features it needs are available fast enough to act on, and propensity scores are already computed.
  • Separate Conversational Intent from Automated Execution. Let the model read what the marketer wants. Let deterministic code decide what gets spent: rules, budget caps, and privacy policies are enforced outside the model, before any reward is triggered.
  • Decouple Event Triggering from Impact Measurement. Trigger in real time. Measure on the category's own purchase cycle, which runs far slower.
  • Build on Zero-Copy Data Foundations. Point the agent at the warehouse itself. Every copy you make is another pipeline to keep in sync and another thing to break.
  • Design for Governed Clean-Room Collaboration. Settle on one privacy-safe way to share, so a brand partner can co-fund a campaign without raw customer data ever leaving the warehouse.
  • Speed vs. Statistical Rigor. Small audiences are noisy, so the agent matches a lookalike baseline and checks the minimum detectable effect before a fast campaign counts as valid.
  • Tiered Autonomy. The agent decides on its own when the call is low-risk, and halts for human sign-off when the liability is high.

Stage 1 · Signal Detection

The engine tells you what's worth acting on

Nobody has to type a goal to get started. The engine scans the member base and returns an Opportunity Feed of ranked signals. The demo curates four, with real queried numbers: two large Craft Soda groups, big enough to measure on their own, plus smaller Organic Snacks and Artisanal Coffee groups that need a comparison group. Each signal carries its own pre-computed cohort, so the agent works on exactly the group shown on the card.

Responsible autonomy

Act on the small calls, escalate the big ones

Before anything runs, the agent checks the play against the governance policy:

Pattern A

Handled automatically

Too small but low cost → it adds a look-alike comparison group itself.

Pattern B

Needs your decision

Small group → it pauses and offers one-click actions: add a comparison group, widen the audience, or skip the test.

Pattern C

Good to go

Group is big enough. Proceed with a normal hold-back comparison group.

Pattern D

Test skipped

Direct send, no comparison group, no readout. The trade-off is stated plainly.

The principle: decide the low-risk calls, escalate the consequential ones. When a human is pulled in, they answer by clicking, not typing.

Clear statistics

Predictions, precision, and the line to significance

The prediction panel forecasts four outcomes: Expected Lift, Expected Extra Value, cannibalization, and Points Budget. Each is tagged FORECAST, under a banner that stays on screen and says these are predictions, not real results. Two calls worth naming:

No significance at design time

A p-value is something you measure after the campaign runs, and there is no data yet. Putting one on a forecast would be misleading. So the app shows power and minimum detectable effect instead: the smallest lift this design can reliably catch, and whether the predicted lift clears it. Real significance waits for stage 5.

  • Point estimates vs. precision. Adding a comparison group does not change the headline numbers, because it is the same offer to the same people. What it changes is how confident you can be. So the prediction ranges tighten when a comparison group is added or the audience widens, and the headline numbers only move when the treated group itself changes.
  • Guardrails are first-class. Cannibalization is held under a 3% guardrail; the clean-room export is k-anonymity gated and differential-privacy tokenized (no raw PII).

Development

Process

How it was developed

Built fast and iteratively: something working early, then sharpened against a few firm principles.

  • Simulation-first, so the demo never fails. The deterministic path was built before the live one: the agent's whole flow runs with no API key, so a live presentation can't be broken by a network call, and it costs nothing to show.
  • Statistical honesty as a design constraint. The hardest calls were about what not to claim. Predictions are labelled as predictions, significance is withheld until there is data, and a comparison group moves precision rather than the headline number.
  • Plain English as a rule. Every label and reply is written for a business audience, not a data scientist. Jargon is removed deliberately, with tooltips for the rest.

  • Phase 3: Pre-Flight Guardrails. The system validates sample size against required statistical power and caps cannibalization under 3%.

The data

A real SQL engine, zero setup

The Loyalty Data Engine is an in-process DuckDB table of 1,000 members on a fixed random seed, so every run returns the same numbers. That matters when the demo has to be repeatable in front of a room. Each member carries transactional, behavioral, and zero-party attributes: tier, points balance, category, brand-switch risk, days since last purchase, stated preference, average basket, and historical CLV. The distributions lean toward the demo's hero categories on purpose, so the scenarios land on realistic group sizes.

Deployment & ops

Container, gate, embed

  • Container & host. A Dockerfile runs Streamlit bound to the host port; deployed to Render via a one-click blueprint.
  • Access. A password gate activates only when a secret is set: open locally, gated in production.
  • Embed. A noindex iframe page publishes it under pablocorzo.com/prototypes/pulse/demo: the app runs on Render, the URL lives on the domain. This overview sits one level up, behind its own password.
  • Secrets. Runs in simulation mode with no API key, ideal for a public, gated demo: no cost, nothing to leak.

Versioning & honesty

What it is, and what it isn't

  • Numbers come from synthetic data, and the statistical formulas are illustrative rather than a production engine.
  • Stages 3–5 (activation, monitoring, measured results) are not built. The app is careful to never imply otherwise.
  • It's an unofficial concept demo that uses Kobie's marks for demo purposes, kept private and noindex.
